Guidelines & Consensus Statements

Last updated : 24/06/2026 - 51342 views

Since 1990, ESICM has been committed to producing guidelines and consensus statements from colleagues within the society as well as in collaboration with other international societies to provide the best quality evidence for clinicians at the bedside. These manuscripts have been published in the journal Intensive Care Medicine and other leading journals (endorsement from ESICM does not imply automatic acceptance for publication in the ICM journal).

ESICM has developed a framework for guidance and consensus: you can download it here.

At the end of 2020, the ESICM Executive Committee decided to initiate the development of guidelines.  The society is fortunate enough to benefit from the expertise of renowned specialists and leaders in a substantial variety of topics in intensive care. Therefore, it was decided to make a selection and to begin with four guidelines on ultrasound, antimicrobial, fluids and acute respiratory distress syndrome.

Under development:

  • ESICM Consensus Statement on Human-Centred Partnership in the ICU. The Co-leaders are: Jos Latour (School of Nursing and Midwifery, Faculty of Health, University of Plymouth, UK), Margo Van Mol (Dept. of Intensive Care Adults, Erasmus MC University Medical Center,  Rotterdam, the Netherlands), Jozef Kesecioglu (Dept. of Intensive Care Medicine, UMC Utrecht, The Netherlands) and Emilio Ruiz (Dept. of Intensive Care Medicine, University Clinic Hospital of Santiago de Compostela, Spain). Development in progress.
  • Joint EANM ESICM Consensus on PET- Positron Emission Tomography in Critically Ill patients
  • Guideline about antimicrobial stewardship for severe infections and sepsis. The co-leaders are Jan De Waele (Ghent University Hospital, Belgium) and Garyphallia Poulakou (National and Kapodistrian University of Athens, Greece). Development in progress.
  • Transfusion Guideline part III – reversal of anticoagulants in the bleeding critically ill (A. Vlaar, Amsterdam, The Netherlands).
  • Weaning from Mechanical ventilation (joint guideline with ERS) (co-chairs are John Laffey, Nicole Juffermans, Martin Dres, Paolo Navalesi)
  • Call for ICU nurses: Help shape your profession’s future. Shaping ICU nurses’ future: How an ICU Nurse Envisions her professional role in 2030 (SHINE). Delphi study, part of the Olympus project, with the aim to define and reach a consensus on ICU nurses’ professional roles in 2030 (Katerina Iliopoulou, Past Chair of the ESICM N&AHPs Committee, Honorary Research Fellow, City University, London, UK, Lead researcher at Hellenic National Public Healthcare Organisation). You are invited to complete three Delphi rounds. Round 1 has closed on 10 May 2026. ICU nurses who complete all rounds will be entered into a lottery for 12 free registrations for ESICM’s Annual Congress 2026, Lisbon, Portugal. Click here for more info on SHINE.
